## 1. Summary

**The code is in better shape than in September.** The newer systems read like the reference implementations the rulebook names: locked rows, relative writes, typed aborts, pushes after commit. A scan for the pattern behind the September outage (a helper on the plain connection inside an open transaction) found one instance in 78 service files. All 65 admin routes enforce a permission. No SQL is built from client text. The content follows the XP law almost exactly: hunting is within 1% of its band at every rung and the median bench recipe sits at 1.80 times the band, which is the policy to two decimal places.

**The two serious findings are both in the oldest code.** Woodcutting, mining, foraging and hunting never check where the player is standing (A1, proven). A character in Talador can work any node on the island by sending its id, and can mine another player's unannounced vein. That removes the map from four skills. And a guild officer can accept any application ever sent to their guild, at any time, whatever the applicant has done since (A2, proven): the applicant is pulled out of their current guild, its leader included.

**The content pipeline was quietly broken, and is now fixed.** No snapshot of live content could be exported since the Chronicle shipped, and one taken today could not have been imported. Both are fixed on `main` and CI is green on the first live snapshot since August (section 5).

**The most urgent finding is not in the code.** The live database has no backup of any kind (O1, section 5): the backup script in the repo was never installed on the box, and the host's own backups are off. One failed disk, one bad migration or one mistaken command would end the game for the 110 accounts on it, 33 of whom have played more than a hundred hours.

**As a game:** the foundations are unusually principled and the writing has a real voice. The two things most worth attention are that every skill stops unlocking things between level 8 and level 22, and that a new player's first level-up takes 35 to 65 minutes while the guest trial lasts 60 (section 7).

## 3. Findings

### A. Proven

**A1. Gathering never checks where the player is standing.** High.

`canChopHere` (`services/woodcutting.ts`), `canMineHere` and `canMineVein` (`services/mining.ts`), `canHunt` (`services/hunting.ts`) and `canForageHere` (`services/foraging.ts`) check level and tool only. Their start routes (`routes/actions.ts:57`, `routes/mining.ts:44` and `:101`, `routes/hunting.ts:36`, `routes/foraging.ts:41`) pass the client's id straight through, and the tick never compares the action's node with `players.current_location_id`. `canMineVein` also does not ask whether the vein is visible to the player.

Proof: `apps/server/src/scripts/auditProofs/remoteGathering.ts`, against the real routes and the real tick, on live content:

```
woodcutting: player stands in Talador; tree is in Lanaivale; start -> 200; tick paid Poor Lanai Log +28 xp
mining_rock: player stands in Talador; rock is in Grundagr; start -> 200; tick paid Granite +7 xp
mining_vein: vein 1 is in Origrund, found by player 2, NOT announced; thief stands in Talador and
             GET /mining/veins shows them 0 veins; start -> 200; tick paid Ambren Ore +32 xp; vein now 9/10
foraging:    player stands in Talador; habitat is in Lanaivale; start -> 200; tick paid 2x Onion Seeds +24 xp
hunting:     player stands in Talador; Deer are in Eld Grove; start -> 200; tick paid +48 Hunting xp
```

Run it with `DATABASE_URL=postgres://.../<a database with "audit" in its name and content loaded> JWT_SECRET=x npx ts-node --transpile-only src/scripts/auditProofs/remoteGathering.ts`.

What it costs: travel, Wayfaring and mounts stop mattering for four skills to anyone who opens the browser console. A finder's private vein, the reward mining gives for being present, can be emptied by someone who was never there. Level gates still hold, so nothing is created that could not be earned, but a level 13 woodcutter never needs to walk to Eld Grove again. It also bends world events: an event pays its bonus where the player stands, so a player can stand at an event and work a better node of that skill somewhere else.

Fix: in each `can…` function, load the player's `current_location_id` and refuse unless it equals the node's, habitat's, animal's or vein's `location_id`; for veins also require that the vein is announced or was found by this player. Repeat the check in the tick's repeat branch (checklist item 5). The rule already says presence belongs in the service layer; fishing, trapping, combat, the museum, merchants and the travelling merchant all do it. Section 6 item 1 makes it impossible to forget. Turn the proof into a `race:check`-style scenario that expects refusals.

**A2. An old guild application can be accepted at any time, and takes the player with it.** High.

`POST /guilds/applications/:id/respond` (`routes/guilds.ts:656-698`) checks the officer's permission and that the application belongs to their guild. It does not check that the application is still pending, and it does not check that the applicant is still without a guild. It inserts a `guild_members` row and overwrites the applicant's `guild_id`, `guild_tag` and `guild_role`. None of the membership routes use a transaction.

Proof: `apps/server/src/scripts/auditProofs/guildPoach.ts`, through the real routes:

```
A accepts x -> 200; x: players.guild_id=1 (recruit); guild_members rows: guild 1 as recruit
B accepts x's older application -> 200; x: players.guild_id=2 (recruit);
          guild_members rows: guild 1 as recruit, guild 2 as recruit
y's application was rejected; B accepts it afterwards -> 200; y: players.guild_id=2 (recruit)
c founded guild 3, then B accepts c's old application -> 200; c: players.guild_id=2 (recruit);
          guild_members rows: guild 3 as founder, guild 2 as recruit; guild 3 leader_id is still 7 (c is 7)
```

What it costs: anyone with the invite permission in a guild a player once applied to can move that player into it whenever they like. The player's chat tag changes, they lose their place in their own guild's chat room, and a leader taken this way leaves a guild whose head is recorded as belonging elsewhere. Because `apply` ignores a repeat application (`onConflict(...).ignore()`), an application row lasts for ever, so the list of players who can be taken only grows. The data is also left with two membership rows for one player.

Fix: one transaction; lock the application and the applicant's player row; require `status = 'pending'` and the applicant's `guild_id IS NULL`; flip the status with a conditional update and act only if one row changed (rule 5). When a player joins any guild, close their other pending applications as the invite path already closes other invites. A unique index on `guild_members (player_id)` would make the database refuse a second membership; the migration must list existing doubles and stop, as the non-negative checks did. The same check-then-write shape, with no transaction, is in `create` (`:115`), the invite accept (`:308`), `leave` (`:540`) and `kick` (`:367`); they refuse correctly one request at a time and would benefit from the same treatment.

### B. From reading (each traced to the code; not run)

**B1. Hunting writes arrows as an absolute number.** Medium. `services/gameTick.ts:909-923` reads the arrow stack with no lock and no transaction, then writes `update({ quantity: newQty })`. A drop, trade or deposit of arrows that lands between the read and the write is overwritten, so arrows dropped on the ground come back into the pack. The window is a few milliseconds but it reopens every hunt. This is the September audit's M4, recorded there as already fixed. Fix: `takeItemsWithin` for the arrow (rule 13), and stop the hunt when it returns short.

**B2. Foraging runs on its own timer rule.** Medium. `calculateForageTimer` (`services/foraging.ts:56-64`) takes 0.1 seconds off per level over, where the shared rule in `lib/actionTimers.ts` takes 0.5% per level. It applies the knife after the node's minimum, so a knife can take the timer below `min_timer`, and it never applies the 20-second floor. CLAUDE.md section 8 names one rule and says "never a local copy". At 30 levels over the Sunlit Meadow, the shared rule gives 38 s and foraging gives 42 s, so a forager gains less from levels than every other gatherer. With today's tier 1 knife nothing goes under 20 s; a late-tier knife would. Fix: route it through `calculateTimer` and express the knife as the tool tier or a multiplier before the floor.

**B3. Nine action starts leave `last_timer_seconds` empty.** Low. Checklist item 1. Missing on the first insert in `routes/actions.ts:83` (woodcutting), `routes/mining.ts:70` and `:139`, `routes/carpentry.ts:105`, `routes/smithing.ts:99` and `:162`, `routes/travel.ts:82`, and in the private `startAction` of `services/farming.ts:288` and `services/shops.ts:103`. Tool wear prices a break from that column (`services/toolWear.ts:192`), and an empty one is treated as a 1-second action, which makes the break chance about a hundredth of what it should be. For the looping skills only the first swing is affected. For farming's and the shop's one-shot actions it is every action, so a hoe or a mallet past its guarantee effectively never breaks. `services/shops.ts:103` also omits `bot_check_pending: false` (checklist item 4).

**B4. Changing gear during a fight is not part of the fight.** Medium, and a design decision. `routes/equipment.ts` and `services/loadouts.ts` never look for a running fight, and a settle replays every swing since the last settle with whatever is worn when it runs (`services/combatFights.ts:263`, `loadWorn`). Wear is charged at the kill to what is worn at that instant (`:331-342`). Two consequences: taking the weapon off for the last blow means it never wears, and a script could hold a two-handed weapon for its own swings and a shield for the creature's. The first is proven (`apps/server/src/scripts/auditProofs/fightWearDodge.ts`: two fighters, one kill each, the blade kept on is counted one use and the blade taken off is counted none); the second was not run. Neither is reachable by hand at a useful rate. Decide whether gear is locked for the length of a fight, or whether equip settles the fight first and costs a swing.

**B5. Opposite lock orders between a cast and a kill.** Low. A cast locks `player_talar` and then `player_stats` through `awardXp` (`services/spells.ts:1053-1063`). A kill by a Keeper's Band wearer locks `player_stats` through `awardXp` and then `player_talar` through `wearItem` and `jewelryAid` (`services/combatFights.ts:295-338`, `services/durability.ts:71`). The same player casting as a kill settles can deadlock; Postgres ends one of the two after a second. If the fight loses, the tick leaves its five-minute lease and the next settle skips the gap. Fix: have the cast take the player row first, as the fight does.

**B6. One plain-connection read inside a transaction.** Low. `resolveVigil` (`services/spells.ts:979`) calls `playerSkillLevel(playerId, 'Talar')` without passing `trx` while holding the player row. It is a read, so it cannot deadlock on a lock, but it borrows a second pool connection inside the first (rule 18). Pass `trx`. A scan of every transaction body and every helper that takes a transaction found no other case.

**B7. Travel reads the location before it settles the fight.** Low. `routes/travel.ts:22-62` reads `current_location_id`, validates the road, then calls `endFightIfAny`. If that last settle kills the player they respawn in Talador, but the journey still starts on the road they chose and they arrive beside the place they died. Re-read the player after the settle.

**B8. A journey can mark a finished trade cancelled.** Low. `routes/travel.ts` finds an active trade and then runs an unconditional `update({ status: 'cancelled' })` with no lock. If the other player's accept commits in between, the completed trade is recorded as cancelled although the goods moved. Nothing is lost, but the trades tables are the evidence the suspicious-trades tool in `docs/IDEAS.md` would read. Make it `WHERE status IN ('pending','active')` under the trade row lock. `stepIntoWithin` in `services/spells.ts:230-253` is the same job done properly for portals and the Daily Step (one transaction, the trade row locked, the action removed, pushes after commit); travel could call it.

**B9. Six item awards still read, then increment or insert.** Low. `services/woodcutting.ts:229-243`, `services/drops.ts:16-24`, `services/foraging.ts:168-174`, `services/farming.ts:240-244` and `services/gameTick.ts:825-831` and `:938-944` look for the stack, then either increment it or insert a new row, outside any transaction. If the player drops or deposits the whole stack between the two steps, the increment changes nothing and the item is lost. If another add creates the row first, the insert breaks the unique key, the resolve throws, and the action sits on its five-minute lease. `addItemToInventory` in `services/inventory.ts` is the atomic upsert written for exactly this (rule 13).

**B10. Push sign-ups have no cap and no check on where they point.** Medium. `routes/push.ts` accepts any `https://` address as a device and stores as many as a player sends. `sendNotice` (`services/pushNotify.ts:154-176`) then posts to every one of them at once, with no timeout, and writes a row per success. So a player can make the server send requests to any HTTPS host they name, in the thousands, every time one of their notices fires, and each send that succeeds queues an update on the ten-connection pool. Fix: allow only the push services browsers really use (`fcm.googleapis.com`, `updates.push.services.mozilla.com`, `*.notify.windows.com`, `web.push.apple.com`), cap devices per player at a handful, and give the send a timeout. Only matters where push is switched on.

**B11. Disband checks the storehouses outside its transaction.** Low. `routes/guilds.ts:793-828` asks `guildStorehousesHoldAnything`, then deletes the guild in a separate transaction. A deposit that lands between the two is deleted with the hall. The comment says the check exists so that disbanding never destroys what members gave; a lock on the halls inside the transaction would make that true.

**B12. The site sends no security headers.** Medium. Checked on live, 2026-11-10: the response for `https://talaran.net/` carries no `Strict-Transport-Security`, no `X-Frame-Options` or `Content-Security-Policy`, and no `X-Content-Type-Options`. The Express app sets none either (`index.ts`; only the avatar mount sends `nosniff`), so nothing in front of the game adds them. The one that matters most here is framing: any website can load the game inside its own page, where a logged-in player's session is live, and lay its own picture over the Drop, Trade and Accept buttons. Fix in nginx, which serves the client: `Content-Security-Policy: frame-ancestors 'none'`, `X-Frame-Options: DENY`, `X-Content-Type-Options: nosniff`, `Referrer-Policy: strict-origin-when-cross-origin`, and `Strict-Transport-Security` (or switch HSTS on in Cloudflare). One trap: in nginx an `add_header` inside a `location` block replaces every `add_header` set above it, and the `location = /index.html` block already has one for `Cache-Control`, so the new headers must be repeated in that block or they will be missing from the one page that needs them. A full script policy is worth doing later and needs care around the Paddle checkout.

**Fixed on the box, 2026-11-10 (Nathan).** The five headers live in `/etc/nginx/snippets/talaran-security-headers.conf`, included from the server block and again from the `location = /index.html` block, and `curl -sI https://talaran.net/` now returns all five. The previous file is at `/root/talaran-nginx.backup-2026-11-10`. Copies of both files as they stand on the box are now in `ops/nginx/`.

Smaller points from the same reading:

- Forgot-password answers the same words for every address but waits for the email to send only when the account exists, so response time gives the answer away. Its cooldown map (`routes/auth.ts:183`) is never pruned.
- The weekly highscore snapshot decides the week by the server's local day (`services/weeklySnapshot.ts:33`), where everything else uses the game's own clock (`lib/gameTime.ts`, New York time).
- An id too large for an integer column reaches Postgres and comes back as a 500 on most routes. Harmless, but it is noise in the log a player can make at will.
- The same whole-number check is written four ways (`parseQuantity`, `count`, `asInt`, inline `Math.floor(Number(x))`).
- The admin content browser hides `players.password_hash` but leaves `reset_token`, `token_version` and `is_admin` readable and editable. That is by design while there is one admin; it stops being safe the day there are two.
- A trade request never expires and is made with a check and then an insert, no lock (`routes/trades.ts:68-140`). A request the other player ignores keeps both of them "already in a trade" until one cancels, travels or disconnects, so a player standing at the Trading Post can be kept from trading by anyone who keeps asking. Two requests sent together can also put one player in two trades; the accept still checks and locks every item, so nothing is duplicated.
- There is still no block or ignore list for whispers and private messages. Private messages also ignore a chat mute and have no limit of their own beyond the general 600 requests a minute (`routes/messages.ts:72`), so a muted player can still write to anyone, as fast as they like.
- `mod_permissions.can_moderate_forum` is read nowhere. Every moderator, whatever their flags, can pin, lock, edit and delete on the forum and read staff-only categories (`routes/forum.ts:661`, `:695`, `:714`, `:766`).
- The poll export (`responsesCsv`, `services/crierPolls.ts:703`) quotes commas and line breaks but leaves a cell that starts with `=`, `+`, `-` or `@` as it is. A player's write-in beginning that way is run as a formula when the CSV is opened in a spreadsheet. Put an apostrophe in front of such cells.
- A shared palette's name is shown to other players with its owner's name and does not pass the language filter (`routes/palettes.ts:105`). Guild names, tags and shop names do.

### C. Content against its own laws (live snapshot)

**C1. The level 13 tree was never rebalanced.** `20260714174215_xp_rebalance_node_values.ts` sets `xp_reward: 49` on a node named `Old Growth Lanai Tree`. The node is called `Deep Lanai Tree`, the update matched nothing, and nothing threw. Live still pays 65 XP per 60 s, 3,900 an hour against a band of 2,926: 33% over. This is the "silent no-op" CLAUDE.md section 2 forbids. Fix with a forward migration that throws if the row is missing.

**C2. Ore nodes pay 32 XP, and that is intended; the documents say otherwise.** Decided by Nathan, 2026-11-10: ore is not always available, so it sits above the band on purpose, and stone sits below it. Live is right. What is wrong is the record: `docs/xp-rebalance.md` section 6 says the ore nodes were changed to 22 on 2026-09-29, and `20260929010000_vein_xp_from_ore_nodes.ts` carries `XP = 22`. That migration only writes where `xp_reward` is null or 0, so on live it changed nothing, but on a database where the column is empty it would set 22. At 28 s, 32 XP is 4,114 an hour at level 1, 1.87 times the gathering band, against a written ore policy of 1.3. Correct the document's row and its policy figure so the next ore is placed against the real rule.

**C3. The higher tiers exist only as item rows.** 46 items sit in tiers 3 to 9: ores, logs, planks, rocks and tools for each tier. Tier 2's metal is the same: Serph Ore, Dense Serph Ore, Serph Hatchet and Serph Pickaxe have no node and no recipe on live. Nothing is broken by this; it is the size of the gap section 7 describes.

**C4. Text rules.** Live content has no em dashes. "The heart of" appears twice (`locations.description`, `items.description` for the Lanai Tanning Rack). One item is named `Tal's Hope`, against the lore rule; it predates the rule and is used by pen flowers and travel finds.

Checked and sound on live: every `recipes.station` is a real station type; every recipe output exists; every hand tool that can break is on a merchant shelf, and the two that are not (gem chisel, jeweller's pliers) are lent by the public lapidary bench, so the circularity rule holds; no duplicate quest names; price walls clean; the tick has a branch and the client has a case for all 39 action types.

### D. Seen in the browser

**D2. The first visit is heavy.** The production build is one script of 1,130 kB (309 kB compressed) and one stylesheet of 365 kB (56 kB compressed), with no code splitting: a visitor reading the landing page downloads the admin panel, the forum and the Manual editor. ## 4. Examined and found sound

- **Rule 18** (no plain-connection work inside a transaction): one read in 78 service files (B6).
- **Admin routes:** all 65 across `admin.ts`, `adminContent.ts`, `adminChronicle.ts`, `adminCrier.ts`, `adminEvents.ts`, `adminManual.ts` check a permission before doing anything.
- **SQL:** no raw statement interpolates client text. The one interpolated value is a constant.
- **HTML:** one `dangerouslySetInnerHTML` in the client, behind DOMPurify with a fixed tag and attribute list. `players.avatar_url` is only ever written by `services/avatars.ts`.
- **Location checks:** fishing, trapping, combat, museum donations, NPC merchants, the travelling merchant and ground pickup all compare against the player's own row.
- **Guild hall storehouse** (`services/guildHall.ts`): items and gold both ways are locked, relative and validated.
- **Combat numbers:** on the live roster all 13 creatures pay within 15% of the band at their level. The sim and the live fight share `lib/combatMath.ts`.
- **Value writes in services:** every exported function in the shop, workstation, store, Taler, church, buff, property, tally, museum, marketplace, liquid, Talar, equipment, loadout, tanning, trapping and smithing services that moves items or gold does so inside a transaction. The only writes outside one are settings and bookkeeping.
- **The fight's core** (`lib/combatFight.ts`): pure, and all of its state is in the clock it is handed, so settling in two steps gives what settling in one gives. A fight is rescheduled for its next swing, so the 15-second stall rule only fires after an outage.
- **Password reset:** a random 32-byte token, stored hashed, with an expiry, cleared on use, and every session ended.
- **Avatar upload:** format allow-list, a pixel limit, a frame limit, a stored-size limit, re-encoded by the server, ten an hour.
- **Paddle webhook:** signature over the raw body with a timestamp tolerance and a constant-time compare; refuses everything when no secret is set.
- **Sockets:** five handlers, all acting on the id from the verified handshake. Leaving a guild takes the socket out of the guild's room.
- **Profile responses:** explicit column lists; another player's last login and playtime are not sent.
- **Ownership on every route that takes an id:** 95 routes outside the admin, forum, message and guild files take an id from the client; each passes the caller's own id to the query or service that uses it, or (guild forum) sits behind a membership gate and filters by that guild.
- **The Town Crier's polls:** the answer is one transaction on the locked player row with the unique key as the gate; eligibility is checked inside it; players are sent counts only, and what others wrote goes to admins alone.
- **Chat:** every send is rate-limited to 30 a minute, a whisper is classified before the guest and mute rules are applied, and public channels pass the language filter.
- **Trade accept:** one transaction under the trade row lock, the offer version the player saw is checked, and every stack and both purses are locked and re-checked before anything moves.
- **Spells that move a player** (portal, Daily Step, Tether): each settles a fight first and goes through one helper that removes the running action and cancels an open trade under its lock.
- **Feats:** evaluated on a level-up or when the panel is opened, never on every action; awarded by an insert that only one request can win.
- **Public pages** (Chronicle, News, Highscores, Rules, Manual): none calls a route that needs a login, so a logged-out visitor is not bounced.
- **Game time:** one module, one timezone, daylight-saving safe.
- **Background timers:** the tick, playtime, world events, ground items and the Crier run on intervals driven by the database, so a restart loses nothing. The weekly snapshot and the chat prune are bare timeouts, but the snapshot is also checked at boot.
- **Private messages and the forum:** every read, edit and delete checks the owner or staff; staff-only threads are hidden from everyone else on every route.
- **Palettes:** a palette is a list of known colour names mapped to six-digit hex values and nothing else, so a shared one cannot carry a web address or other CSS.
- **The language filter:** linear on hostile input. A 500-character chat line of stretched, starred or spaced letters takes under 3 ms and a 20,000-character post under 90 ms.
- **Client timers and polling:** the action timer runs on server time, and an idle client makes roughly 30 requests a minute against a limit of 600.
- **The backup script's design** (`ops/backup/talaran-backup.sh`): dumps are read back before they are trusted, pruning is by count, the off-site copy never deletes. It was never installed, and had a bug that stopped it before the off-site copy; fixed (O1).

## 5. The content pipeline: broken, fixed, proven

CLAUDE.md records that the committed content snapshot was from 2026-08-08. Asking for a fresh one turned up why it had never been refreshed.

1. **`content:export` failed on live.** It ordered every table by `id`. `tool_durability_tiers` is keyed by `tier` and `chronicle_deed_kinds` by `key`. The export has been unable to finish since the first of those tables was registered.
2. **A fresh snapshot could not have been imported.** The import upserted on `id` (wrong for those two tables and for `durability_tiers`, whose `id` is not unique), and it imported `npcs` before `quests` although `npcs.hide_after_quest_id` points at a quest. CI would have gone red on the first live snapshot.

Fixed in `0b734ff`: `lib/contentKeys.ts` asks the database for each table's real primary key and orders the import by the real foreign keys. Proven: the old export fails with the exact error from live; with the fix, export, import into a database built as CI builds it, import again and export again give two identical exports, and the merchant seed runs on the result. CI is green on the live snapshot (`2aaba6e`).

## 6. Structure: make the conventions enforce themselves

CLAUDE.md says nearly every shipped bug was a convention that already existed and went unread. A1 and B3 are that again. These are ranked by how soon they pay back.

**1. One way to start an action.** Small to medium.
There are 18 inserts into `player_actions`, five private `startAction` or `insertAction` helpers, three copies of `busy()`, 25 existing-action checks and 16 separate catches of `23505`. Nine inserts omit `last_timer_seconds`, one omits `bot_check_pending`, and four routes omit the location check. A single `startAction(player, spec)` in `services/` that takes the place the action happens at, refuses when the player is not there, does the busy check and the `23505` translation, and always writes the timer and bot-check fields would have made A1 and B3 impossible and removes checklist items 1, 3 and 4. It can be adopted one route at a time.

**2. The tick's handler registry.** Medium to large; recommended in September, not built.
`processCompletedAction` is one function of 1,459 lines (the file has grown from 1,493 to 1,858 lines since September). It holds 39 action types, 34 separate deletes of the action row, 27 calls to `emitActionComplete`, 12 copies of the action limiter and 31 hand-built XP summaries. Every new skill adds a branch and re-implements all of it. Move one type at a time behind the claim step that already exists, starting with the newest branches, which are the cleanest.

**3. Unit tests for the pure modules.** Small.
There are no test files. `lib/combatMath.ts`, `combatFight.ts`, `actionTimers.ts`, `toolWear.ts`, `gemGrades.ts`, `crierPollRules.ts`, `wordFilter.ts`, `museumPlacement.ts` and `tiers.ts` are pure and hold the rules most easily broken without notice. Node's built-in test runner needs no dependency. Twenty tests and a CI step would have caught B2 the day the timer rule was written.

**4. Let CI refuse a bypass of a single writer.** Small.
`player_skills` is clean: `awardXp` is the only writer outside the admin grant and new-player setup. `player_inventory` is not: 18 files outside `services/inventory.ts` still write it directly, among them `gameTick.ts` (B1), `woodcutting.ts`, `fishing.ts`, `husbandry.ts` and `trades.ts`. Most are locked. A short script in CI that greps for writes to `player_inventory`, `property_storage`, `ground_items` and `players.gold` outside their owning module, with a committed allow-list that only shrinks, turns "the rest of the inventory module" from a note into a number.

**5. A route wrapper.** Small, mechanical.
Routes hold 314 `try/catch` blocks and 279 hand-written `res.status(500).json({ error: 'Server error' })`. The JSON error handler at the end of `index.ts` already does that job. An `asyncRoute(handler)` wrapper deletes the boilerplate and the chance of a route answering nothing.

**6. One admin gate.** Small.
The six admin routers use six idioms: an inline `is_admin` read, `hasPermission`, `isAdmin`, `requireAdmin`, an `admin()` handler wrapper, and `router.use(requireAuth, adminOnly)`. All 65 routes are correct today. `adminCrier.ts` has the right shape: the gate is on the router, so a new route cannot be added without it.

**7. The client's types.** Medium.
`apps/client/tsconfig.app.json` has `"strict": false`, with 318 `any`. The server has `strict: true` and 502 `any`, 414 of them in services, mostly database rows. `GameLayout.tsx` holds 79 `useState` calls. A shared package of response types, starting with the ten busiest endpoints, would stop the server and client disagreeing silently.

Looked at and not recommended now: an ORM or a query-builder change, splitting the server into services, a client state library. None removes a class of bug this project has actually shipped.

## 7. The game

What follows rests on the live content, the repo's own sims, a short guest playthrough, and reading. It does not rest on live player data, which would confirm or correct the two main points; the queries are at the end of this section.

### What is strong

- **The voice.** Quank's tutorial teaches the timer loop, the half-hour check, the teachers, the Manual and the market, and is funny while doing it. The writing rules in CLAUDE.md are visibly working.
- **Skills need each other by design.** The circularity rule, foraging as the one skill that breaks the circle, husbandry sending players back out for young, food as the only healing: these give players reasons to touch other skills and each other.
- **The economy has a doctrine.** Derived values, the two price walls, pricing attention and not clock time, no repair so that makers always have customers. The walls check clean on live.
- **The social layer works at 30 players.** The Chronicle names people, the museum records who gave what first, the Crier asks players what they think, shops and the guild storehouse work while their owner is away. None of them needs a crowd online at once.
- **The first screen.** The landing page says what the game is in two sentences, the guest path needs no email, and the action card tells a player what they got and how far the next level is.

### Where it is thin

**1. Every skill runs out of new things early.** The level at which each skill last opens anything, on live:

| Skill | Last unlock | Skill | Last unlock |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mining | 1 | Farming | 12 |
| Carpentry | 8 | Woodcutting | 13 |
| Fishing | 9 | Hunting, Husbandry, Crafting | 17 |
| Foraging | 10 | Cooking | 18 |
| Smithing, combat creatures | 12 | Talar | 22 |

By the XP law's own table, level 13 is about 25 hours of one skill at the band and level 25 about 83. Past its last unlock a skill is the same action for hundreds of hours: the law's own projection for a ladder that stops at level 13 is 560 hours to level 50. The repo's farming sim shows the same thing from the other side: a full farm falls from 0.94 times the band at level 12 to 0.32 at level 57, because the taper is waiting for a crop that does not exist. As a rough estimate, the whole game holds about 400 hours in which something new still opens, which at the 8 hours a day CLAUDE.md assumes is about seven weeks.

Mining stands out: all three of its nodes are level 1, so a miner never unlocks anything.

The build order in CLAUDE.md adds skills. The evidence here argues for depth next: one second tier down the main chain (Serph ore, its tools, the level 25 wood) gives a level-up that means something in six or seven linked skills at once, and it is also what makes a player economy possible. While every recipe is reachable by level 18, every player can make everything, and there is little reason to buy from anyone.

**2. The first level-up is an hour away, and the trial is an hour long.** Level 2 needs 2,413 XP in any skill. That is 65 minutes of chopping, 35 of mining a vein on live's numbers, or about 37 of bench work with materials already in hand. A teacher's quest pays 500. A guest who follows Quank's advice and walks to a teacher will usually see the trial end before any skill levels. The XP law chose slow early levels deliberately, and the long curve does not have to change to fix this: paying the first quest of each skill about one level's worth of XP gives away roughly one hour of that skill's 2,920-hour journey and puts the game's main reward inside the first session.

**3. The half-hour check is the most frequent event in the game and gives nothing back.** At 8 hours a day a player answers about sixteen sums. The last audit recorded that the check stops no script, and the decision to leave it was made then. As a limit on unattended play it does its job. It could do the same job as a small moment a player is glad to see: a find, a choice between two small things, a line from the world. The interruption stays; the feeling changes.

**4. Being present is asked for but not used.** Between checks there is nothing to do inside a skill. Mining already has the right idea: a vein rewards the player who is watching. World events do too. More of that kind of thing, short and optional, is what separates this from a game that would be better left closed.

**Point 1 is confirmed, and it is the committed players who are past the ceiling.** Twenty-five players are at or beyond woodcutting's last unlock and thirteen are at 25 or more, on one tree. The top woodcutter is level 54: from 13 to there is somewhere between 230 and 560 hours of the same action, depending on how much of it was at the timer floor. Every one of the 58 miners has been past mining's last unlock since their first swing, and the top is level 45, about 300 hours. In Farming, ten of fifteen are past the last crop and the leader is level 61, deep into the taper. These are the people most worth a second tier.

**Point 2 is confirmed: the first hour is where players are lost, and almost nowhere else.** Of 110 registered accounts:

Combat has been live for under a month, so this may only be early, but it is worth asking players why before building more of it: the cost of dying, the food and gear needed to start, and Georold being the only signpost are the candidates I can see from the code.

## 9. What is left

Everything the server does with items, gold, accounts and other players' data has now been read at least for its gates, and the paths that move value have been scanned or raced. What has not been looked at:

1. **The client's game logic.** `GameView.tsx` and `GameLayout.tsx` hold the action loop, the result card and 108 pieces of state between them, with strict type-checking off. Bugs here show as a stuck or wrong screen, not as lost items, because the server decides everything that matters. This is the largest unread part.
2. **The Chronicle's composer, the world-event scheduler and the museum's display rules.** Their writes are covered; whether they always choose the right verse, event or case is not.
3. **Jewelry and the spell effects that do not move a player** (Wearsight, Larder Ward, Sending, Kindle), beyond the races `race:check` already holds.
4. **Combat as a player would attack it**, beyond the one exploit run (B4): the swing-by-swing gear swap, and whatever a determined player finds in a month.

None of these is likely to hold another finding of the size of O1, A1 or A2. Each fits in one session.
