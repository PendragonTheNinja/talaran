You have the shape of it now. Work takes time, and the time passes whether you watch it or not.

What is left is choosing a trade, which everyone overthinks. Let me make it smaller.

## If you want to be useful immediately

Take up **[Mining](/manual/skills/mining)** or **[Woodcutting](/manual/skills/woodcutting)**.

Both are gathering trades: go somewhere, do the work, be paid. You already carry the tools. And half of Talaran is short of stone, ore or timber at any given moment, so you will never be stuck for something to do with what you bring back.

This is the boring recommendation. It is also the right one, which happens more often than anybody admits.

## If you want to build things

Then the chain runs like this: cut timber, saw it into planks and parts, raise something from them. **[Carpentry](/manual/skills/carpentry)** is the hinge the rest turns on. Farmsteads and workbenches both begin there, and it will make you popular in a way mining never quite does.

## If you want to be paid in stories

**[Hunting](/manual/skills/hunting)** has the best tales in it, and I collect tales for a living. You track something, you take the shot, and sometimes you miss. Very little else in Talaran lets you miss. I find it bracing.

On Taiar, speak to **Geonsen** at Eld Grove.

## If you want to grow something

Go to **Novita**, find **Georgic**, and read the [Farming](/manual/skills/farming) page. Know before you start that farming is mostly a matter of going away and coming back, and that this is harder than it sounds.

## The thing I would tell my younger self

Do not specialize yet.

The trades are tied together so closely that trying several of them early is groundwork, however much it feels like dithering. A hunter who has never cut timber has no shafts for his arrows. A farmer who cannot build stands about waiting for someone else's planks.

Try four things badly. By the fourth you will see how they feed one another, and you will have stopped minding the "badly."

Then choose, if you still want to. Many people find they no longer need to.

## One more thing, for later

Taiar Island is where all of us begin, and for now it is the whole of the map. It will not always be.

I mention it so that you will hold your plans loosely. What you learn of the trades here holds everywhere, and will travel with you. The towns, groves and outcrops in this manual are Taiar's own, and one day they will be the first chapter of several.

I intend to be insufferable about having written the first chapter.
