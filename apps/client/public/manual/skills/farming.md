Let us begin with a confession: I was a dreadful farmer.

Oh, I *understood* farming. I understood it the way a man falling off a cliff understands gravity, which is to say thoroughly and far too late to be useful. I had come from the forests, where patience means swinging until the tree gives up, and from the forges, where patience means waiting for metal to stop being angry. Soil, I assumed, would be a third variety of the same virtue.

Soil does not care what you assume. Soil has one lesson and it delivers it to everyone identically: *you cannot hurry this, and you should go away.*

## Where and how to start

Farming begins with property, which surprises people who expected it to begin with a seed.

You do not sow a field on common ground. You raise a **farmstead** first, and the fields come after. On Taiar, that means **Novita**, and it means speaking to **Georgic**, who teaches the whole sequence in the proper order. That is considerably more than I had, and he does it without once mentioning that you might have asked sooner.

Raising the farmstead is a serious piece of joinery: five hundred planks, five hundred blocks of granite, a thousand nails, and the better part of ten minutes' honest work. Note that it pays **Carpentry** experience rather than Farming, and pays it generously. I have always thought that a fair acknowledgement of what it costs a man.

## The work, in order

A field turns the same wheel every time, on any island you ever farm:

1. **Enclose** it, with fencing and a stone border. Three minutes.
2. **Till** the soil, ninety seconds, the only step that strictly demands a hoe in your hands.
3. **Sow** your seed, a few seconds each, ten seeds to a full field.
4. **Wait.** The crop grows in real time, whether you are present or not.
5. **Harvest**, a touch slower than sowing, and the step that finally pays you.

Between step four and step five you should be somewhere else entirely. That is not a suggestion for the efficient. That is the design.

## Tools and gear

- **Hoe, must be equipped.** In your hands, to till. Owning one accomplishes nothing.
- **Lanai Mallet, must be equipped.** In your hands, to raise a farmstead or fence a field.
- **Ambren Saw, carried.** It need only be in your pack when you build.
- **Lanai Bucket, carried.** It need only be in your pack when you tend.

The rule beneath the rules is a sensible one: if the tool is the thing doing the work, it must be in your hands. If it is bench kit standing by, carrying it is enough.

## Soil, and the great argument about it

Soil sits in one of three conditions, depleted, ordinary, or rich, and it governs **how much you harvest and nothing else whatsoever**.

Your experience does not move. The work was the same work; only the field's generosity differed. I have heard men argue otherwise at length and with feeling. They were wrong at length and with feeling.

A hungry crop drags the soil down a step when you pull it. Legumes lift it, being the only genuinely polite plants. A field left alone recovers on its own, roughly a step every eighteen hours. Manure lifts it at once, if you have any and can bear to part with it.

Rich soil is worth about a quarter again over ordinary. Depleted costs you nearly half. Neither is worth a moment's panic.

## Tending

Tend a field with a bucket and you shave a tenth off whatever growing time remains. Fifteen seconds a field, and across a large farmstead it accumulates into something that looks almost like cleverness.

It is the closest thing to a trick this trade permits, and I want you to notice how modest it is. Farming does not reward cleverness. Farming rewards *turning up*.

## Connections

Farming does not stand alone, and was never meant to.

- **Carpentry** builds everything you farm upon, the farmstead and every fence panel. Raise it alongside Farming, or buy from someone who did.
- **Mining** supplies the granite bordering every field.
- **Cooking** is where the grain goes, and the reason to grow more than you can eat.
- **Husbandry** shares your land and your manure, and each trade makes the other cheaper.

If you are wondering which to raise first: the Carpentry, then the farm. Do it the other way about and you will spend a memorable afternoon standing in Novita, waiting on planks, composing a document.
