Let us begin with a confession: I was a dreadful farmer.

Oh, I *understood* farming. I understood it the way a man falling off a cliff understands gravity: thoroughly, and too late to be useful. I had come from the forests, where patience means swinging until the tree gives up, and from the forges, where patience means waiting for metal to stop being so angry. Soil, I assumed, would be a third variety of the same virtue.

Soil does not care what you assume. It has one lesson, and it gives it to everyone the same way: *you cannot hurry this, and you should go away.*

## Where and how to start

Farming begins with property, which surprises people who expected it to begin with a seed.

You do not sow a field on common ground. You raise a **farmstead** first, and the fields come after. On Taiar that means **Novita**, and it means speaking to **Georgic**, who teaches the whole business in the proper order. That is a good deal more than I had, and he never once mentions that you might have asked sooner.

Raising the farmstead is a serious piece of joinery: five hundred planks, five hundred blocks of granite, a thousand nails, and ten minutes of honest work. It pays **Carpentry** experience rather than Farming, and pays it well, which seems only fair given what it costs. Just don't forget a mallet and a saw.

## The work, in order

A field goes through the same steps every time, on any island you ever farm:

1. **Enclose** it, with fencing and a stone border. Three minutes.
2. **Till** the soil. Ninety seconds, and the one step that needs a hoe in your hands.
3. **Sow** your seed, a few seconds each, ten seeds to a full field.
4. **Wait.** The crop grows in real time, whether you are there or not.
5. **Harvest**, a little slower than sowing, and the step that finally pays you.

Between steps four and five you should be somewhere else entirely. The trade is built that way.

## Tools and gear

- **Hoe, equipped.** In your hands, to till. One in your pack does nothing.
- **Mallet, equipped.** In your main hand, to raise a farmstead or fence a field.
- **Saw, equipped.** In your off hand, beside the mallet, whenever you build.
- **Bucket, carried.** It need only be in your pack when you tend.

## Soil, and the great argument about it

Soil is depleted, ordinary, or rich, and that governs **how much you harvest and nothing else**.

Your experience does not move. The work was the same work, and only the field's generosity differed. I have heard men argue otherwise at length and with feeling. They were wrong at length and with feeling.

A hungry crop drags the soil down a step when you pull it. Legumes lift it a step, being the only polite plants. A field left alone recovers by itself, about a step every eighteen hours. Manure lifts it at once, if you have any and can bear to part with it.

Rich soil yields about a quarter more than ordinary. Depleted soil yields two fifths less. Neither is worth panicking over.

## Tending

Tend a field with a bucket and you take a tenth off whatever growing time remains. It costs fifteen seconds a field, and across a large farmstead it adds up to something that looks almost like cleverness.

That is the nearest thing to a trick this trade allows, and it is a small one. Farming rewards *turning up*.

## Connections

Farming was never meant to stand alone.

- **Carpentry** builds everything you farm on, the farmstead and every fence panel. Raise it alongside Farming, or buy from someone who did.
- **Mining** supplies the granite that borders every field.
- **Cooking** is where the grain goes, and the reason to grow more than you can eat.
- **Husbandry** shares your land and your manure, and each trade makes the other cheaper.

If you are wondering which to raise first: Carpentry, then the farm. Do it the other way about and you will spend a memorable afternoon in Novita, waiting on planks and composing a document.
