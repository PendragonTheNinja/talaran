I like mining because a rock has never once lied to me.

You hit it. It breaks or it doesn't. I took it up straight after a season among merchants, kept at it for two years, and have rarely been so well rested.

One thing to know before you start, because nearly everyone works it out late. **Stone is the steady wage. The veins hiding under it are why you came.**

## Where and how to start

Find a stone outcrop. Equip a pickaxe. That is the whole entrance examination.

Every outcrop states the level it expects of you and the grade of pickaxe it would prefer. Work within your means at first. An outcrop that wants a better pickaxe than yours will not turn you away. It will just take its time, at your expense.

## Rock first, ore second

Strike an outcrop and you get **stone**, plus experience. Dull, dependable, and every builder in Talaran wants it.

Each strike also carries a chance of something better: a **vein**, a real seam of ore, ambren or burgh or whatever the ground there has been hoarding. Work a vein and you take home metal instead of more gravel.

Now pay attention, because this next part has cost me dearly and need not cost you anything.

**A vein you discover is yours alone for ten minutes.** After that, everyone at that location can see it, and anyone may work it. A vein holds only so much ore, and it does not refill.

So when you find one, *work it*. Do not nip back to town. Do not, as I once did, return a borrowed axe first.

I have lost more good ambren to tidiness than to any rival I ever had.

## Dense ore

Once you are fifteen levels clear of what an ore demands, you will now and then pull **dense** ore instead of the ordinary sort. The chance climbs steadily from there, and at forty-five levels over it stops being a chance. Every piece comes out dense.

This is the argument for going back to ore you have decided is beneath you. A master working humble ambren is not wasting his afternoon. Every swing pays him better than it pays you.

## Tools and gear

Grade matters in both directions, and the two directions are not equal.

- A pickaxe **above** the required grade speeds the work, up to about a third faster at three grades over. The tool will never give you more than that.
- A pickaxe **below** grade costs you dearly. Each grade short adds roughly forty percent to the time.

So the lesson is not "always carry the finest pickaxe." It is **never work an outcrop with a pickaxe beneath it.** Arriving under-equipped costs far more than arriving over-equipped gains, which is true of a great many things besides mining.

## Connections

- **Smithing** is where the ore goes. The two are really one long trade, and a miner who never smiths ends up selling raw metal to someone who read further than he did.
- **Crafting** wants the gemstones that mining turns up in passing.
- **Carpentry** and **Farming** both eat granite by the cartload, for every fence and every farmstead border. On Taiar this makes a miner permanently welcome in Novita.
- **Woodcutting** is Mining's twin: same rhythm, same rules about tools, different material. Whichever you learn first teaches you most of the other.
