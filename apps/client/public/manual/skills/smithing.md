Every trade in Talaran wants something from the smith. The smith wants ore, fuel, and to be left alone. That is the strongest bargaining position in the world, and smiths know it.

Mind the middle item on that list. Ore alone will not do. A forge is a fire, and a fire eats.

## Where and how to start

Smithing begins with a **workstation**.

You need three things in your pack:

- An **Anvil**
- A **Hammer**
- **Tongs**

Take them to the place you mean to work and fit them. The forge exists from the moment the first one goes down, and from then on it belongs to that place. The tools stay there when you leave. You may lift one back out if you must, but the forge is cold until all three are in.

That is the difference between a tool and a *place*.

## Ore and fuel

Smelting takes ore **and Charc**, and a smith who has stocked one without the other has stocked nothing.

I dwell on this because it is the commonest way a new smith stalls: a full pack of ore, a cold forge, and no idea why. Charc is fuel. Fuel gets burned. You will need more tomorrow.

## Climbing

The trade rises in tiers, and each tier wants ore of a matching grade, so Smithing and Mining climb together. A smith who has outpaced his miner has excellent technique and a cold forge.

{{tab:The Kiln}}

The kiln is the part of smithing that works while you are away, and new smiths overlook it for far too long. I certainly did. I bought Charc for a year like a man buying water beside a river.

## What it does

A kiln burns logs into **Charc**. You load it, go away, and come back to fuel.

The smith will show you the kiln once he has taken you on, and you may keep **one burn going at each place** you work. Load it and leave it to its slow business while you are at the forge, or the mine, or anywhere else at all.

## The arithmetic, which is worth knowing exactly

Logs go in **twenty at a time**. That is one batch, and the kiln will not take part of one.

**The quality of the log decides the yield**, and by a good margin:

- Poor logs: **60 Charc** a batch
- Fine logs: **80 Charc** a batch
- Excellent logs: **100 Charc** a batch

Read those with a woodcutter's eye. Excellent logs give two thirds more Charc than poor ones, for the same twenty logs and the same three hours of waiting. There is no stronger argument in Talaran for a smith to raise his own Woodcutting, because timber improves with a woodcutter's level and stone never does.

**A burn takes three hours**, however many batches you loaded. One batch or five, the kiln is done in three hours.

So load it full. A kiln burning one batch is wasting most of a day.

## How much you may load

Your Smithing level decides how many batches the kiln will take at once:

- One batch to begin with
- Two at Smithing 10
- Three at 20
- Four at 30
- Five at 40

At level 40 with excellent logs, that is five hundred Charc from one three-hour burn. At level 1 with poor logs it is sixty. Same kiln, same three hours.

Every batch also pays some experience, so a full kiln pays twice: Charc for the forge, and about a fifth of a smith's progress, earned while he was somewhere else.

## What I would do

Load the kiln before you do anything else, every time you arrive somewhere you work.

It costs you the logs and a moment's thought, and it runs while you are busy. The smith who does this never has to stop work to go and buy fuel. The smith who does not will find his forge cold at the worst possible moment, and more than once.

## Tools and gear

Nothing need be held. Smithing is bench work, and the bench does the holding. What matters is having the workstation, with ore and Charc to hand.

## Connections

Smithing sits in the middle of nearly everything, which is why it is worth the cost of setting up.

- **Mining** feeds it entirely. The one is not much use without the other.
- **Woodcutting** feeds the kiln, and log quality decides your fuel yield.
- **Hunting** needs arrowheads. Every arrow in Talaran passes through a forge.
- **Carpentry** needs nails by the thousand. A single farmstead wants a thousand of them.
- **Crafting**, **Farming**, and **Husbandry** all want tools and fittings that begin here.

If you have ever wondered why smiths seem to know everyone, it is not charm. Everyone eventually needs a nail.
