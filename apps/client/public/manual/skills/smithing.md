Every trade in Talaran wants something from the smith, and the smith wants only ore, fuel, and to be left alone. It is the most powerful negotiating position in the world, and smiths know it.

Note the middle item on that list. Ore alone will not do. A forge is a fire, and a fire eats.

## Where and how to start

Smithing begins with a **workstation**.

You need three things, and you must be carrying all of them:

- An **Anvil**
- A **Hammer**
- **Tongs**

Take them to a location and set up. The three are **consumed** in the setting up. They become the workstation; you do not get them back, and you do not carry them about afterwards.

This is not theft, whatever it feels like the first time. It is the difference between a tool and a *place*.

## Ore and fuel

Smelting takes ore **and Charc**, and a smith who has stocked one without the other has stocked nothing.

I labour the point because it is the commonest way a new smith stalls: a full pack of ore, a cold forge, and no notion why. Charc is fuel. Fuel gets burned. You will need it again tomorrow.

## Climbing

The trade rises in tiers, and each tier wants ore of a matching grade, so Smithing and Mining climb in lockstep. A smith who has outpaced his miner is a smith standing beside a cold forge with excellent technique.

{{tab:The Kiln}}

The kiln is the part of smithing that works while you are not there, and new smiths overlook it for far too long. I certainly did. I bought Charc for a year like a man buying water beside a river.

## What it does

A kiln burns logs into **Charc**. You load it, you go away, and you come back to fuel.

The smith will show you the kiln once he has taken you on, and you may keep **one burn going at each place** you work. Load it, leave, and let it do its slow business while you are at the forge or the mine or anywhere else at all.

## The arithmetic, which is worth knowing exactly

Logs go in **twenty at a time**. That is one batch, and the kiln will not take a part of one.

**The quality of the log decides the yield**, and the difference is not small:

- Poor logs: **60 Charc** a batch
- Fine logs: **80 Charc** a batch
- Excellent logs: **100 Charc** a batch

Read those again with a woodcutter's eye. Excellent logs return a **two-thirds** greater yield than poor ones for the same twenty logs and the same three hours of waiting. This is the single strongest argument in Talaran for a smith to raise his own Woodcutting, because timber quality climbs with a woodcutter's level while stone quality never does.

**A burn takes three hours**, and here is the part that matters: three hours regardless of how many batches you loaded. One batch or five, the kiln is done in three hours.

So load it full. A kiln burning one batch is a kiln wasting most of a day.

## How much you may load

Your Smithing level decides how many batches the kiln will take at once:

- One batch to begin with
- Two at Smithing 10
- Three at 20
- Four at 30
- Five at 40

At level 40 with excellent logs, that is five hundred Charc from a single three-hour burn. At level 1 with poor logs it is sixty. The same kiln, the same three hours.

Every batch also pays some experience, so a full kiln pays you twice over: charcoal for the forge, and a quiet passive fifth of a smith's progress.

## What I would actually do

Load the kiln before you do anything else, every time you arrive somewhere you work.

It costs you nothing but the logs and a moment's thought, it runs while you are busy, and the smith who does this reliably never once has to stop work to go and buy fuel. The smith who does not will spend his life discovering that his forge is cold at the worst possible moment.

## Tools and gear

Nothing need be held. Smithing is bench work and the bench does the holding. What matters is having the workstation, and having ore and Charc to hand.

## Connections

Smithing sits at the middle of nearly everything, which is why it is worth the setup cost.

- **Mining** feeds it entirely. These two are one trade wearing two hats.
- **Woodcutting** feeds the kiln, and log quality decides your fuel yield. 
- **Hunting** needs arrowheads. Every arrow in Talaran passes through a forge.
- **Carpentry** needs nails, and needs them by the thousand. A single farmstead wants a thousand of them.
- **Crafting**, **Farming**, and **Husbandry** all want tools and fittings that begin here.

If you have ever wondered why smiths seem to know everyone, it is not charm. It is that everyone eventually needs a nail.
