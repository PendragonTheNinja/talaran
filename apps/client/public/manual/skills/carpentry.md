If you learn one trade properly, learn this one.

I say that having tried them all, and having been mediocre at most. Carpentry is the hinge Talaran turns on. The farm is built of it, and so is the tannery. Arrows start as carpentry, and half the workstations of every other trade came out of somebody's saw. A world made of wood needs somebody to work the wood, and that somebody is never short of work.

## Where and how to start

There are two ways to work timber, and you will use both.

**A public bench** asks only permission. On Taiar, the bench at **Verdale** opens to you once you have done the carpenter's introductory work, and from then on it lends you everything except the saw in your hand.

The catch is that public benches are slow. Everything takes twice as long there as it would at your own. That is the price of using somebody else's tools, and it is a fair one.

**Your own workstation** is the real answer. You will want one as soon as you can afford it.

## Setting up your own bench

The bench itself wants two things:

- A **Sawhorse**, which becomes the bench
- A **Plane**, fitted to it for the work that wants one

Both go into the bench when you set up. Like the smith's anvil, the bench then belongs to the place you built it and does not travel with you.

The **Saw** is another matter. It stays in your hand. Sawing logs, and every other job that calls for a saw, wants one equipped, at your own bench or anybody else's.

## The two halves of the trade

Carpentry divides cleanly, and it is worth knowing which half you are doing.

**Sawing** turns logs into planks. It is repetitive and fast, and everything else rests on it.

**Woodworking** turns parts into things: tools, furnishings, workstations for other trades, the fittings a farm needs.

Most people find they enjoy one half a good deal more than the other. Both pay.

Sawing is the trade. Building is what the trade is *for*.

Somewhere around the fourth hundred planks, a carpenter stops thinking in boards and starts thinking in structures. It is a pleasant moment. Enjoy it, because what follows is a great deal of counting.

## The difference between making and raising

A plank is made. You stand at a bench, spend a log and receive a board, and if you walk away afterward the board comes with you.

A homestead is raised. You spend a large quantity of everything and receive a place, and the place stays exactly where you put it for as long as it exists. Nothing built in this section can be picked up, moved, sold, or regretted into a refund. Choose the ground before you choose the timber.

## What asks for what

Two of these are carpentry outright. Two more are farming's and two are the stockman's. But every one of them is raised with a saw in hand and boards on the ground, so they are all listed here, not scattered across four pages by whichever trade claims the level requirement.

The **tally board** is the cheapest thing on the list and the first most people build. It asks for Carpentry level 5 and a modest pile of boards and nails (and it can go up almost anywhere).

How many you may keep is a separate question from whether you can build one. Everyone may keep one. Beyond that you buy a license in coin, and the town will only sell you one if your total level across every trade is high enough to suggest you have something worth watching. A person who has spent three hundred hours perfecting one skill in one place is welcome to their mastery, but they do not need six boards to tell them about it.

The **shop** at Talador is the first serious undertaking. It asks very little of your level and a great deal of your stores.

The **homestead** is the largest single expense in Talaran as I write this: five hundred boards, five hundred blocks of granite, and a thousand nails. I have watched people work for a fortnight toward it. I have also watched them build it and at once begin planning the next thing, which tells you something about people.

**Plots**, **coops**, and **paddocks** are the fittings that go on a homestead afterward, and they share an awkward habit: each one costs more than the last. The second coop is dearer than the first, the sixth dearer still. This is deliberate. Better to plan around it than to discover it.

## A word on the nails

Every structure here wants nails, and the larger ones want more of them than you expect. A thousand for a homestead. Seven hundred for a shop.

If you intend to build, make a friend at a forge before you make a plan. I have known perfectly good carpenters stopped dead by a shortage of the smallest item on the list, standing on a mountain of their own timber with nothing to fasten it.

## Connections

Carpentry has more of these than any other trade, which is the argument for learning it.

- **Woodcutting** feeds it entirely. Learn both, or befriend someone who did.
- **Smithing** supplies the nails, and a farmstead alone wants a thousand of them.
- **Farming** cannot begin without you. Every farmstead and every fence panel is carpentry.
- **Crafting** needs the tanning rack and barrel.
- **Hunting** needs arrow shafts.
- **Carpentry itself** builds its own sawhorse, which pleases me more than I can justify.

A carpenter is never selling to one market. When farming is in fashion he sells fences, and when hunting is in fashion he sells shafts. He is the one tradesman in Talaran who cannot really have a bad season.
