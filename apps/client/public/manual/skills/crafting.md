Crafting is the trade that catches everything the other trades drop.

Some work belongs neither to the smith, who deals in metal, nor to the carpenter, who deals in timber. Someone must take the hide off the hunter's hands and make something of it. Someone must cut the gemstone the miner turned up and did not want. That someone is a crafter. Crafting looks like a miscellany because it is one.

I mean that as praise. A trade that makes one kind of thing stays narrow. A trade that takes in whatever needs doing keeps finding more to do.

Crafting runs in disciplines, each with its own rhythm. Tanning is the patient one. The lapidary rewards a steady hand.

## What Crafting is for

Look at what the other trades end up holding and cannot use.

The hunter has hides. The miner has gemstones and a shrug. The farmer has flax. None of those is a finished thing, and the trade that produced it has no use for it. Crafting is where they become goods.

So Crafting is **almost never the first trade in a chain and almost always the last.** A crafter with no suppliers has an empty bench. A crafter who hunts, or who knows a hunter, has the most profitable arrangement I know of.

## The disciplines

Each discipline has its own tempo and its own equipment. They share a level, so raising Crafting anywhere raises it everywhere.

At present there are two, **Tanning** and the **Lapidary**, each with a tab of its own. Others will follow, and this page will grow tabs as they do.

## Level does more than open recipes

Worth knowing early: in some disciplines your Crafting level buys *capacity* as well as access. Tanning is the clearest case. Levels there give you more vats running at once, not faster vats.

I have come to prefer that kind of reward. Speed makes you quicker at the same job. Capacity changes the job.

Tanning taught me to stop watching.

I have said elsewhere that Talaran rewards the person who starts a thing and walks away. In tanning there is nothing else to do. You load, you leave, and you come back.

## Setting up a tannery

Tanning wants two pieces of equipment:

- A **Lanai Tanning Rack**, for the scraping and the stretching.
- A **Lanai Tanning Barrel**, for the bark-liquor soak.

Both are built by Carpentry, and both stay where you set them up, as the smith's anvil does.

Put the tannery where the hides are. Hides come from hunting, hunting happens in forests, and carrying things is the hidden tax on every trade in Talaran.

## How a soak works

Load a vat with hides. Walk away. Come back to buckskin.

A vat takes **ten hides** at a time, and the soak runs on a fixed schedule. You need not be present, awake, or in Talaran at all. When it finishes, it waits. Nothing spoils for being collected late.

## More vats

Your Crafting level governs how many vats you may run at once:

- One vat to begin with
- A second at Crafting 10
- A third at 20
- A fourth at 30
- A fifth at 40

Each vat soaks its own hide type on its own clock. A tanner at Crafting 40 is not tanning five times faster. He is running five staggered soaks, loading one while another finishes, and the waiting has turned into scheduling.

## Buckskin and leather are not the same thing

This trips people up, so plainly:

**Buckskin** comes from wild animals you hunted, and what you get depends on the beast.

**Leather** comes from cattle you raised through Husbandry, and it runs in proper tiers.

They are separate materials with separate uses. A recipe asking for one will not accept the other, however alike they look hanging on a rack.

## The bench

A lapidary wants two tools to cut and a third to set:

- A **Lanai Lap Wheel**, built by Carpentry: a wheel faced with ground granite, turned by a treadle.
- An **Ambren Gem Chisel**, forged by Smithing, for splitting a stone along its grain.
- **Ambren Jeweller's Pliers**, also forged, for closing a setting over a stone. Cutting does without them. Setting does not.

On Taiar, **Geosper** keeps a public lapidary in Caliwen and lends all three to anyone who has done his lesson. It runs at half speed, as every public bench does. Your own runs at full.

## Cutting

Hold a rough stone to the wheel and it comes off as one of three grades:

- **Cut**, most of the time.
- **Polished**, more often as your level climbs past the stone's.
- **Flawless**, rarely, and a good deal less rarely for a practiced hand.

The grade matters because the light cares about it. A Polished stone holds half again what a Cut one does, and a Flawless one holds three times as much. The Talar page has the whole of that.

## Setting

Once a stone has been filled at the Rift, a crafter can set it in Ambren as a ring or an amulet. Each piece takes **three Lit, two Bright or one Radiant** stones, so a cutter who turns out a Flawless stone needs only one of them, and a cutter who never does needs three. Either way the piece comes out the same.

What each piece does is on the Talar page, since the light is what makes them work.

## Connections

- **Hunting** supplies the hides, and is the reason a hunter should never sell a raw skin.
- **Husbandry** supplies the cattle, and so the tiered leather.
- **Mining** turns up the gemstones, and **Talar** fills them once you have cut them.
- **Carpentry** builds the racks, barrels, and benches every discipline needs.
