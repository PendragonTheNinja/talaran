Crafting is the trade that catches everything the other trades drop.

Some work does not belong to the smith, who deals in metal, nor to the carpenter, who deals in timber. Someone must take the hide off the hunter's hands and make it into something. Someone must set the gemstone the miner turned up and did not want. That someone is a crafter, and the reason Crafting looks like a miscellany is that it genuinely is one.

I mean that admiringly. A trade defined by what it makes will always be narrow. A trade defined by *usefulness* has no ceiling at all.

Crafting runs in disciplines, each with its own rhythm. Tanning is the one you will meet first.

## What Crafting is for

Look at what the other trades hand you and cannot use.

The hunter has hides. The miner has gemstones and a shrug. The farmer has flax. None of those are finished things, and none of them belong to the trade that produced them. Crafting is where they become goods.

This makes Crafting unusual in one important way: **it is almost never the first trade in a chain and almost always the last.** A crafter with no suppliers is a crafter with an empty bench. A crafter who hunts, or who knows a hunter, is running the most profitable arrangement in Talaran.

## The disciplines

Each discipline has its own tempo and its own equipment. What they share is a level, so raising Crafting anywhere raises it everywhere.

At present the discipline in the world is **Tanning**, described on the next tab. Others will follow, and this page will grow tabs as they do.

## Level does more than unlock recipes

Worth knowing early: in several disciplines, your Crafting level governs *capacity* rather than just access. Tanning is the clearest case, where levels buy you more vats running at once rather than faster vats.

That is a different kind of reward, and I have come to prefer it. A bigger number makes you quicker. More capacity makes you a different sort of worker.

Tanning taught me to stop watching.

I have said elsewhere that Talaran rewards the person who starts a thing and walks away. Tanning is where that stops being advice and becomes the entire mechanism. There is no active tanning. There is loading, and there is leaving, and there is coming back.

## Setting up a tannery

Tanning wants two pieces of equipment rather than one:

- A **Lanai Tanning Rack**, for the scraping and the stretching.
- A **Lanai Tanning Barrel**, for the bark-liquor soak.

Both are built by Carpentry, and both are **consumed** when you set the tannery up, exactly as the smith's anvil is. The tannery then stays where you put it.

Put it where the hides are. Hides come from hunting, hunting happens in forests, and carrying things is the hidden tax on every trade in Talaran.

## How a soak works

Load a vat with hides. Walk away. Come back to leather.

That is the whole of it. A vat takes **ten hides** at a time, the soak runs on a fixed schedule, and it does not require you to be present, awake, or in the world at all. When it finishes it simply waits. Nothing spoils for being collected late.

## More vats, more patience in parallel

Your Crafting level governs how many vats you may run at once:

- One vat to begin with
- A second at Crafting 10
- A third at 20
- A fourth at 30
- A fifth at 40

Each vat soaks its own hide type on its own clock. So a tanner at Crafting 40 is not tanning five times faster. He is running five staggered soaks, loading one while another finishes, and turning a trade about waiting into a trade about scheduling.

That transformation is the real reward for the levels, and it is a more interesting reward than a bigger number.

## Buckskin and leather are not the same thing

A distinction that trips people up, so let me be plain.

**Buckskin** comes from wild animals you hunted, and what you get depends on the beast.

**Leather** comes from cattle you raised through Husbandry, and it runs in proper tiers.

They are separate materials with separate uses. A recipe asking for one will not accept the other, however similar they look hanging on a rack.

## Connections

- **Hunting** supplies the hides, and is the reason a hunter should never sell a raw skin.
- **Husbandry** supplies the cattle, and therefore the tiered leather.
- **Mining** turns up the gemstones.
- **Carpentry** builds the racks, barrels, and benches every discipline needs.
