Crafting is the trade that catches everything the other trades drop.

Some work does not belong to the smith, who deals in metal, nor to the carpenter, who deals in timber. Someone must take the hide off the hunter's hands and make it into something. Someone must set the gemstone the miner turned up and did not want. That someone is a crafter, and the reason Crafting looks like a miscellany is that it genuinely is one.

I mean that admiringly. A trade defined by what it makes will always be narrow. A trade defined by *usefulness* has no ceiling at all.

Crafting runs in disciplines, each with its own rhythm. Tanning is the patient one; the lapidary is the one that rewards a steady hand.

## What Crafting is for

Look at what the other trades hand you and cannot use.

The hunter has hides. The miner has gemstones and a shrug. The farmer has flax. None of those are finished things, and none of them belong to the trade that produced them. Crafting is where they become goods.

This makes Crafting unusual in one important way: **it is almost never the first trade in a chain and almost always the last.** A crafter with no suppliers is a crafter with an empty bench. A crafter who hunts, or who knows a hunter, is running the most profitable arrangement in Talaran.

## The disciplines

Each discipline has its own tempo and its own equipment. What they share is a level, so raising Crafting anywhere raises it everywhere.

There are four, each with a tab of its own: **Tanning**, the **Lapidary**, **Sewing**, which makes armor and cloaks out of whatever a creature left behind, and the **Camp Work**, the small useful things that need no bench at all. On Taiar, all of it is done in **Caliwen**. Antura has no craftworks of its own, so its crafters come home to Caliwen to work, which is a long way to carry a Snallygaster's scales and you will do it anyway.

## Level does more than open recipes

Worth knowing early: in several disciplines, your Crafting level governs *capacity* rather than just access. Tanning is the clearest case, where levels buy you more vats running at once rather than faster vats.

That is a different kind of reward, and I have come to prefer it. A bigger number makes you quicker. More capacity makes you a different sort of worker.

Tanning taught me to stop watching.

I have said elsewhere that Talaran rewards the person who starts a thing and walks away. Tanning is where that stops being advice and becomes the entire mechanism. There is no active tanning. There is loading, and there is leaving, and there is coming back.

## Setting up a tannery

Tanning wants two pieces of equipment rather than one:

- A **Lanai Tanning Rack**, for the scraping and the stretching.
- A **Lanai Tanning Barrel**, for the bark-liquor soak.

Both are built by Carpentry, and both are set into place where you mean to tan, exactly as the smith's anvil is. The tannery then stays where you put it. Neither wears out.

Put it where the hides are. Hides come from hunting, hunting happens in forests, and carrying things is the hidden tax on every trade in Talaran.

## How a soak works

Load a vat with hides. Walk away. Come back to buckskin.

That is the whole of it. A vat takes **ten hides** at a time, the soak runs on a fixed schedule, and it does not require you to be present, awake, or in the world at all. When it finishes it simply waits. Nothing spoils for being collected late.

## More vats, more patience in parallel

Your Crafting level governs how many vats you may run at once:

- One vat to begin with
- A second at Crafting 10
- A third at 20
- A fourth at 30
- A fifth at 40

Each vat soaks its own hide type on its own clock. So a tanner at Crafting 40 is not tanning five times faster. He is running five staggered soaks, loading one while another finishes, and turning a trade about waiting into a trade about scheduling.

That transformation is the real reward for the levels, and it is a more interesting reward than a bigger number.

## Buckskin and leather are not the same thing

A distinction that trips people up, so let me be plain.

**Buckskin** comes from wild animals you hunted, and what you get depends on the beast.

**Leather** comes from cattle you raised through Husbandry, and it runs in proper tiers.

They are separate materials with separate uses. A recipe asking for one will not accept the other, however similar they look hanging on a rack.

## The bench

A lapidary wants two tools to cut and a third to set:

- A **Lanai Lap Wheel**, built by Carpentry: a wheel faced with ground granite, turned by a treadle.
- An **Ambren Gem Chisel**, forged by Smithing, for splitting a stone along its grain.
- **Ambren Jeweller's Pliers**, also forged, for closing a setting over a stone. Cutting does without them; setting does not.

On Taiar, **Geosper** keeps a public lapidary in Caliwen and lends all three to anyone who has done his lesson. It runs at half speed, as every public bench does. Your own runs at full.

## Cutting

Hold a rough stone to the wheel and it comes off as one of three grades:

- **Cut**, most of the time.
- **Polished**, more often as your level climbs past the stone's.
- **Flawless**, rarely, and a good deal less rarely for a practised hand.

The grade matters because the light cares about it. A Polished stone holds half again what a Cut one does, and a Flawless one holds three times as much. The Talar page has the whole of that.

**Garnet** is Antura's stone, from Underway's granite, and cuts from **Crafting 13**. It takes a Serph chisel to cut at its stated pace, as quartz takes an Ambren one.

## Setting

Once a stone has been filled at the Rift, a crafter can set it in metal as a ring or an amulet: quartz in Ambren, and from **Crafting 20**, garnet in **Serph**, for the second tier of the same six pieces. Each piece takes **three Lit, two Bright, or one Radiant** stones, so a cutter who turns out a Flawless stone needs only one of them, and a cutter who never does needs three. Either way the piece comes out the same.

What each piece does is on the Talar page, since the light is what makes them work.

A few pieces want no light at all, only a part of something that was killed for it: the **Knuckle Ring** and the **Jackalope Charm** on Taiar, the **Heartstone Ring** and the **Kelpie's Bridle Charm** on Antura. They lend aim or power to a fighter, worn on a finger or at the neck, and the Melee page explains what those words mean.

{{tab:Sewing}}

Every creature that fights back leaves something tougher than itself behind: a pelt, a plate, a scale. A crafter sews those into armor, and it is the only way an island's armor is made. Nothing worth wearing drops whole off anything.

## The sets

- **Ratskin** on Taiar, the beginner's set, from the rats everyone starts on.
- **Crabshell** on Taiar, sewn from the plates of the shore's armored things.
- **Snallyscale** on Antura, sewn from **Snallyscales**: Gauntlets at **Crafting 14**, then Sabatons, Helm, Shield, and Greaves, to the Cuirass at **21**. It is worn from **Defense 19** to **24**, a piece at a time.
- Cloaks: the **Wolfskin Cloak** on Taiar, and Antura's **Bearverine Mantle**, from Cave Bear and Wolverine pelts, at **Crafting 20**.

Sewing a piece and wearing it are different gates. Crafting makes it; Defense decides who may put it on. The Defense page lists every piece and what it turns aside.

{{tab:Camp Work}}

Some of the most useful things in Talaran need no bench at all. A knife, a bit of string, and a quiet half minute.

- **Leather Strips**, cut from leather, which half the armor and tools in the world want a length of.
- **Nettle Cordage**, from **Crafting 5**: two stinging nettles twisted into rope in thirty seconds. It is the rope in every boat, and a boat wants a great deal of rope.
- **Snares**, for the trapper; **Fishing Nets**, for the angler; the **Halter & Lead**, for the stockman with a horse to bring in.
- The **Straw Skep**, which is how bees come home; the **Foraging Basket** and **Leather Foraging Gloves**.
- **Granite Blocks**, dressed from raw granite, which every builder in Talaran wants by the hundred.

None of these is glamorous. Every one of them is the thing somebody is short of on the day it matters.

## Connections

- **Hunting** supplies the hides, and is the reason a hunter should never sell a raw skin.
- **Husbandry** supplies the cattle, and therefore the tiered leather.
- **Mining** turns up the gemstones, and **Talar** fills them once you have cut them.
- **Carpentry** builds the racks, barrels, and benches every discipline needs, and buys your cordage by the boatload.
- **Combat** brings you every pelt, plate, and scale an armor set is sewn from.
- **Foraging** finds the nettle and the flax.
