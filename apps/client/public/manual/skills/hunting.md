Hunting takes animals for meat, hides, and feathers, in two ways: the hunt, with bow and arrows, and trapping, with snares you set and come back to. A hunt can fail, so you can come home empty-handed.

## Where and how to start

On Taiar, go to **Eld Grove** and talk to **Geonsen**. When his lesson begins he gives you a **Lanai Hunting Bow** and 100 **Ambren Arrows**.

To hunt an animal you need:

- A **bow**, equipped in your main hand.
- **Arrows** in your pack.
- The **level** the animal asks for.

Choose **Hunting Grounds** at the location, then pick an animal.

## How a hunt goes

Each hunt tracks the animal, stalks it, and takes the shot, then ends in a kill or a miss.

New animals open every eight levels, three to a forest, and each forest has its own. On Taiar you start with deer at Eld Grove; boar and larger game come later.

## Your chance of a kill

- Each level above the animal's requirement adds **0.5%**.
- The arrow's tier changes it (below).
- **The chance never goes above 95%.** One hunt in twenty can always miss.

## Arrows

**You shoot the best arrow you are carrying.** Its tier against the animal's:

- **One tier below** the animal: **−10%** to your chance.
- **One tier above**: **+3%**.
- **The same tier**: no change.

If your arrows are too weak, the hunt tells you before you start. **Serph Arrows** are made at the forge in Emberra from a Serph Ingot, five Lanai Arrow Shafts, and two Feathers, the same way as Ambren Arrows.

**Most arrows come back:**

- After a **kill**, you recover about **80%** of the arrows shot, plus 1% for every two levels above the requirement, up to **95%**.
- After a **miss**, you recover **30%**, whatever your level.

## The bow

A bow sets the pace. A bow one tier above the animal makes each hunt 10% faster, up to 30% at three tiers over. A bow of the animal's tier or below hunts at the normal pace. The bow never changes your chance of a kill.

Besides Geonsen's, the Lanai Hunting Bow can be made with Carpentry.

Trapping catches small animals while you do something else. Set a snare, leave, and come back to collect.

## How a trap works

A trap is in one of three states:

- **Set:** waiting.
- **Sprung:** it holds a catch, waiting for you to collect it.
- **Scavenged:** you left a sprung trap too long. The **perishable** parts of the catch (the meat) are gone; durable parts usually remain.

Collect sprung traps reasonably often and you lose nothing.

## What a snare catches

{{data:trap-targets}}

**Nesting Hen** and **Wild Sow** matter most: chicks and piglets for Husbandry come only from snares. Together they are about one catch in eight.

## Bait

Bait makes one animal more likely. Put it in a snare when you set it, and the animal that likes that bait becomes **eight times** as likely; everything else stays as likely as before.

- **Nesting Hen**: about one catch in twelve unbaited, better than two in five baited.
- **Wild Sow**: one in twenty-five unbaited, one in four baited.
- Rare catches stay rare, but become realistic.

**Trapping uses the same bait pouch as fishing.** Break down grain, cheese, eggs, spawn, or raw meat in the trapline panel, as you would at the water. Spawn is scarce, so spending it on snares means less for fishing.

- **One bait per snare**, used when you set it.
- **Bait is not returned**, on collecting or on taking the snare down.
- **Bait raises the odds; it does not guarantee the catch.**

## How many traps you can run

- One to start
- Two at Hunting 12
- Three at Hunting 25
- Four at Hunting 37

## Traps break

Each collection has a chance of breaking the trap. Keep spare snares.

## What it is for

Trapping is slower than hunting and catches smaller animals, but it is the only source of chicks and piglets, so Husbandry depends on it.

## Connections

- **Carpentry** and **Smithing** make your arrows (shafts and heads).
- **Husbandry** starts here: chicks come from the Nesting Hen and piglets from the Wild Sow, both caught in snares.
- **Crafting** tans hides into leather and makes leather goods.
- **Cooking** uses the meat.
