A hundred kilometres, end to end. That is Taiar Island, and I want you to sit with the number a moment, because it is the single most underestimated fact about the place.

You will spend more of your life on the roads than in any workshop, at any outcrop, in any field. New arrivals treat travel as the dull bit between the interesting bits. Then they look up one evening and realise the dull bit *was* the evening.

So let us make the roads cheap. Everything on this page is a world rule and will follow you to whatever islands Talaran opens next. Only the hundred kilometres belongs to Taiar.

## Two ways to cross, and two skills to match

Everything about travel divides along one line. Are you on your own feet, or on something else's?

**On foot**, the relevant virtue is **Agility**, and it is a generous one. Every level of Agility shaves a full one percent off your journeys. Permanently. Everywhere.

**Mounted**, the virtue is **Equitation**, and it is gentler at half a percent a level. But you begin from a vastly better place, because the mount has already done the heavy work before your skill is consulted at all. Your starting pony makes the trip in a third of the time your legs would charge.

The two do not stack. Ride, and Agility waits in the stable.

## The floor

Now the important part, and the part that saves people from a doomed ambition.

**A journey can never fall below a tenth of its base time.** Not with the finest mount in Talaran, not with a lifetime of Equitation, not with every scrap of travel gear ever forged. One tenth, and no further.

I have met men who set out to break that floor as a life's work. I liked them enormously. They were wrong, and their journeys were exactly as long as everyone else's.

The floor exists so that distance means something. A world you can cross instantly is not a world, it is a menu.

## Gear that helps on foot

Certain equipment trims your journeys directly: a good staff in your hands, decent boots on your feet. These add together, and they add on top of Agility.

Your starting **Lanai Staff** does this. It is a modest three percent, and I would forgive you for dismissing it, but three percent of every journey you will ever take is not a modest number at all. It is a modest-*looking* number, which is a different thing and a more interesting one.

## Experience is paid on the honest distance

Here is my favourite thing about travel in Talaran, and I have thought about it more than is healthy.

**You are paid for how long the journey would have taken, not how long it took you.**

Get faster and you lose nothing. The road that once cost you an hour still pays as though it cost you an hour, forever. Every improvement you make is pure profit, and the world never claws it back.

I find this quietly moral. Most systems in most worlds punish you for getting good at them.

One wrinkle. Riding pays at a lower rate than walking, so Equitation earns rather less per journey than Agility does. Consider it the price of comfort. You are, after all, sitting down.

## What I would actually do

Ride everywhere. Ride from the first hour.

The mount's advantage is so much larger than anything your skills will contribute for a long while that walking in order to train Agility is, in nearly every case, a false economy. You will train Agility eventually, on the trips where you have no mount to hand, and it will feel like finding money in an old coat.

And travel is passive. Set out, then go and do something else while the road happens. A man watching his own journey progress is a man who has read none of my other pages.
