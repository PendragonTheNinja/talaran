A hundred kilometers, end to end. That is Taiar Island, and it is the most underestimated fact about the place.

You will spend more of your life on the roads than in any workshop, at any outcrop, in any field. New arrivals treat travel as the dull bit between the interesting bits. Then they look up one evening and realize the dull bit *was* the evening.

So let us make the roads cheap. Everything on this page is a world rule and will follow you to whatever islands Talaran opens next. Only the hundred kilometers belongs to Taiar.

## Two ways to cross, one skill to learn

You are either on your own feet or on something else's. Either way you are learning **Wayfaring**.

**On foot**, every level of Wayfaring takes a little more off your journeys, everywhere and for good. The early levels give the most. The later ones keep giving, a little less each time, for as long as you keep at it.

**Mounted**, the beast has done the heavy work before your skill is consulted at all. Each mount has a pace it starts at and a pace it can be coaxed down to, and Wayfaring closes the gap between the two. The rouncey, for one:

## The floor

Now the part that saves people from a doomed ambition.

**A journey can never fall below a tenth of its base time.** Not with the finest mount in Talaran, a lifetime of Wayfaring, and every scrap of travel gear ever made. One tenth, and no further.

I have met men who set out to break that floor as a life's work. I liked them enormously. Their journeys were exactly as long as everyone else's.

The floor is there so that distance means something. A world you can cross in an instant is only a menu.

## Gear that helps on foot

Some equipment trims your journeys directly: a good staff in your hands, decent boots on your feet. These add together, and they add on top of Wayfaring.

A plain **Lanai Staff** does this. It is a modest three percent, and I would forgive you for dismissing it. But it is three percent of every journey you will ever walk, and a small number that applies forever is worth checking twice.

Travel gear must be **worn**: held in your hand or on your feet. A staff in your pack is a stick.

## Experience is paid on the honest distance

This is my favorite thing about travel in Talaran, and I have thought about it more than is healthy.

**You are paid for how long the journey would have taken, not how long it took you.**

Get faster and you lose nothing. The road that once cost you an hour still pays as though it cost an hour, forever. Every improvement is yours to keep.

Most systems in most worlds punish you for getting good at them. This one does not, and I think the better of it.

One wrinkle. Riding pays at a lower rate than walking, so the same road earns rather less Wayfaring from the saddle. Consider it the price of comfort. You are, after all, sitting down.

Walkers see more, too. A rider passes the odd thing in the verge at a trot. A walker stops and picks it up.

## What I would do

Ride when you need to be somewhere. Walk when you don't.

That is most of it. A journey on horseback is over sooner, and the time it saves goes back to the bench or the rock face. A journey on foot pays better for the same road and turns up the odd find besides. Both feed the same skill, so no trip is wasted, and nobody need agonize over which way to go.

And travel looks after itself. Set out, then go and do something else while the road happens. A man watching his own journey progress has read none of my other pages.
