There is no board in the square.

I open with that because every new arrival spends their first days looking for one: some notice-board, some tidy list of Available Work with a queue beside it. It does not exist. I checked thoroughly, and then I checked again in a different town, in case Novita was simply being rude.

Talaran does not advertise its work. Its work *stands about*, looking unremarkable, waiting to be spoken to.

## Quests are conversations

Every piece of work begins with a person. You find them, you talk to them, and if they judge you capable (they are generous judges) they offer you something to do.

So a quest you have not been offered does not appear anywhere: not in a list, not grayed out, not marked on a map. The conversation has not happened yet, so there is nothing to show.

That makes finding work an activity in itself. I think this a good thing, though I concede I would, being a man who talks to people for a living.

## Therefore: talk to anyone standing still

That is the whole technique. Anyone motionless in a town is worth a word.

Some will have nothing for you. Some will have nothing for you *yet*, so come back when you know more. And some have been standing there the whole time, holding the exact thing you needed a week ago.

The ones who teach the trades share a naming habit you may notice. On Taiar, **Geonsen** at Eld Grove teaches hunting and **Georgic** at Novita teaches farming, and there are others in the same mold. They are all of a school. It was, if you will permit an old man a moment of vanity, *my* school, and I intend to see it planted on every island Talaran ever opens. Georgic was the patient one, which is presumably why he ended up in a field.

## What a quest asks of you

Work is made of small honest tasks, tracked as you go. Build the thing, till the ground, hunt the animal, gather the goods, or just go and speak to someone.

Two conveniences worth knowing.

**Progress you have already made counts.** Accept a task to till a field when you have been tilling fields all morning, and the world credits what you have already done. Nobody enjoys doing work twice for a witness.

**Turning in is a conversation too.** You go back to the person who sent you and tell them. There is no form to file. Someone asked you to do a thing, and now you get to say you did it, which is most of the pleasure of doing things.

## A word on rewards

Quests hand over goods and experience, and the tutorial ones hand over the tool you will need for the trade they teach.

But the real reward of a tutorial quest is that somebody walks you through a trade in the right order before you invent a worse one. I did not have this. I invented a great many worse orders. Several are still spoken of in low voices at taverns.

Go and talk to someone.
