It took me longer than it should have to see the most interesting thing about Talaran.

**No trade here can stand alone. Not one.**

This goes further than saying a specialist is inefficient. Follow any trade far enough and it stops, and waits, and will not go on until some other trade hands it something. That is deliberate, and once you have seen it you see it everywhere.

## Follow an arrow

Take one arrow, of the sort a hunter loses in the brush without much thought.

The head is metal, so a smith made it. The smith needed ore, so a miner dug it. The shaft is wood, so a carpenter split it. The carpenter needed a log, so a woodcutter felled it. The carpenter also needed a bench, which is itself carpentry, built from timber somebody else cut.

Five trades, one arrow, and the hunter who looses it is the only one of the five not making anything.

Then the hunter's kill becomes hides for the crafter, meat for the cook, and young animals for the herder. The herder's cattle become better leather than the wild ever gave, and the leather comes back around as gear for the people who started the whole business.

It is a wheel, and you are standing somewhere on it.

## What this means for you

**Early breadth beats early depth.** Someone at level twenty in five trades can supply himself. Someone at level ninety in one is a specialist with a shopping list, and spends more time haggling than working.

**The shortage is rarely where you look for it.** A hunter who cannot afford arrows does not have a hunting problem. He has a smithing problem, or a woodcutting problem, and no amount of hunting will fix it.

**Every gathering trade has more customers than it thinks.** A miner assumes he sells to smiths. He also sells to every carpenter building a farm border, every farmer enclosing a field, and everyone raising a workstation. Granite goes further than ore does.

**Pick the trade upstream of the one you want.** If you want to hunt, learn carpentry first. If you want to farm, learn carpentry first. If you want to smith, learn mining first. The trade you want will still be there in a fortnight, and you will arrive able to supply yourself.

You will notice how often that advice ends in carpentry. The reason is on the [Carpentry](/manual/skills/carpentry) page.

## The web, roughly

Read this as "feeds into":

- **Woodcutting** feeds Carpentry.
- **Mining** feeds Smithing, Carpentry, Farming, and Crafting.
- **Carpentry** feeds Farming, Crafting, Hunting, and its own workstations.
- **Smithing** feeds Hunting, Carpentry, Farming, and every trade needing a tool.
- **Hunting** feeds Crafting, Cooking, and Husbandry.
- **Husbandry** feeds Crafting and Cooking.
- **Farming** feeds Cooking and Husbandry.
- **Foraging** feeds Cooking, Farming, and Crafting.
- **Crafting** takes from nearly everyone and hands back gear.

**Cooking sits at the end of almost every line.** It hands back no materials, only meals, and somebody has to be the reason for all the rest.

## The argument for guilds, made arithmetically

If a full chain is five trades and you are one person, you have two choices: learn five trades, or find four people.

Four people is faster. It always has been. That is why [guilds](/manual/systems/guilds) exist, and why I would join one long before you feel you have earned it.

The wheel turns better with more hands on it.
