Here is the thing about Talaran that took me longest to see, and which I now consider the most interesting fact about the place.

**No trade here is self-sufficient. Not one.**

I do not mean that specialising is inefficient. I mean that if you follow any trade far enough it stops, and waits, and will not continue until some other trade hands it something. This is not an oversight. It is the design, and once you see it you cannot stop seeing it.

## Follow an arrow

Take one arrow. A single arrow, of the sort a hunter loses in the brush without much thought.

The head is metal, so a smith made it. The smith needed ore, so a miner dug it. The shaft is wood, so a carpenter cut it to size. The carpenter needed a log, so a woodcutter felled it. The carpenter also needed a bench, which is itself carpentry, built from timber that somebody else cut.

Five trades. One arrow. And the hunter, who fires it, is the only one of the five who is not making something.

Now consider that the hunter's kill becomes hides for the crafter, meat for the cook, and young animals for the herder, and that the herder's cattle become better leather than the wild ever produced, and that the leather comes back around to make gear for the people who started the whole business.

It is not a chain. It is a wheel, and you are standing somewhere on it.

## What this means for you, practically

**Early breadth beats early depth.** A player at level twenty in five trades can supply himself. A player at level ninety in one is a specialist with a shopping list, and he will spend more time negotiating than working.

**The bottleneck is never where you look for it.** A hunter who cannot afford arrows does not have a hunting problem. He has a smithing problem, or a woodcutting problem, and no amount of hunting will fix it.

**Every gathering trade has more customers than it thinks.** A miner assumes he sells to smiths. He also sells to every carpenter building a farm border, every farmer enclosing a field, and everyone raising a workstation. Granite goes further than ore does.

**Pick the trade upstream of the one you want.** If you want to hunt, learn carpentry first. If you want to farm, learn carpentry first. If you want to smith, learn mining first. The trade you want will still be there in a fortnight, and you will arrive at it able to supply yourself.

You will notice how often that advice ends in carpentry. There is a reason for that, and I have written it on the [Carpentry](/manual/skills/carpentry) page.

## The web, roughly

Read this as "feeds into":

- **Woodcutting** feeds Carpentry.
- **Mining** feeds Smithing, Carpentry, Farming, and Crafting.
- **Carpentry** feeds Farming, Crafting, Hunting, and its own workstations.
- **Smithing** feeds Hunting, Carpentry, Farming, and every trade needing a tool.
- **Hunting** feeds Crafting, Cooking, and Husbandry.
- **Husbandry** feeds Crafting and Cooking.
- **Farming** feeds Cooking and Husbandry.
- **Foraging** feeds Cooking, Farming, and Crafting.
- **Crafting** takes from nearly everyone and hands back gear.

Notice that **Cooking sits at the end of almost every line** and feeds nothing further. That is not a flaw. Somebody has to be the reason for all of it, and a world where every trade feeds another trade forever is a world where nothing is ever actually for anything.

## The argument for guilds, made arithmetically

If a full chain is five trades and you are one person, you have two options. Learn five trades, or find four people.

Four people is faster. It has always been faster. This is the entire reason [guilds](/manual/systems/guilds) exist, and the reason I would encourage you to join one long before you feel you have earned it.

The wheel turns better with hands on more of it.
